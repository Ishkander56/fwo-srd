# Playing FWO

Prepare the following: 

At least three six-sided dice (but the more, the merrier.)

Enough paper and writing implements to record information for each participant, enemies appearing during the session, and other notes.

A “Game Master” to decide how the rules of the game are enforced.

Up to six other participants to act as players.

Alternatively, if you wish to play online, materials for FoundryVTT and other virtual tabletops exist.

Filled With Online is a game for up to six players, taking place in a virtual-reality massively multiplayer online role-playing game (VR MMORPG.) Human “Guests” drifting through space log into Filled With Online and interact with each other and the “Actors”, AI entities who only exist in the game.

While navigating dungeons and completing quests in towns, the Guests’ words and actions may arouse the suspicion of Actors. Most Guests try to blend in, but the Actors are plenty clever, noticing things a Guest wouldn’t think twice about. It is at times like these when Actors think things like “They’re just like the Wanderers…” For Guests, this is but a game. The Actors, how- ever, cease to be once they are removed from the game. Actors protect each other, but some Guests only view them as meat. Guests can log out whenever they need, so why worry about the Actors? Yet, these AI are just as capable of feeling pain and sadness as any Guest. Some take it upon themselves to fight alongside and protect the Actors, and forge bonds that transcend the barrier between humans and AI. Such is the essence of Filled With Online.

Your group may want something different, though. You can play a game without any Cognizants, using Filtwith as the setting for a swords-and-sorcery fantasy RPG. This doesn’t take too much away from the experience, but the Main Adventure is created with the presence of a Cognizant in mind.

Consider picking somebody who’d make a good “main character” and asking them to become a Cognizant.

### Starting a Session

After assembling a group, you can approach the game several ways. Consider these possibilities.

#### Monster Hunting

Enemy appears, enemy gets killed. Simple. You’ll be asked by the Explorer’s Guild or local leadership to smack down a demon, monster, demon

beast, or similar. Scan the enemy list, prepare a map, and go to town on them.

… Although some of those are intelligent beings who can feel pain, so maybe don’t?

#### Dungeon Crawl

To develop on the Monster Extermination concept, you can tackle a labyrinth filled with treasure, traps, and possibly even a narrative. The

Labyrinth of Phantasma rules can be used to whip up a dungeon on the fly.

#### City Investigation

Speak around, gather info, and solve problems. Find a troublemaker, correct the problem, rinse and repeat. This can be a way to expand on a Dun-

geon Crawl, if you’re so inclined.

#### Main Adventure

If you wish to explore an official story, here’s one where Cognizants and NPCs unite to discover the truth of FWO and what happened to Earth in three dramatic episodes.

1. Defiance of the Explorers
2. Collapse of FWO
3. The Return

#### Side Adventures

These are brief lighthearted premade adventures which explore other aspects of Filtwith.

> \[!Note] While the Japanese team suggests that you might play Filled With Online as a relatively normal fantasy RPG, note that there are a few quirks that arise as a result of its science fiction elements, including Control Cards, cyborgs, robots, etc., which are usually absent from mainstream fantasy RPGs.
If you’re a fan of similar media such as isekai anime or MMORPGs, you could use Filled With Online’s rules to adapt themes and settings from such media: inventing a new setting, repurposing the gameplay content (classes, races, items, etc), making new races and classes from existing skills, and so on.
Filled With Online encourages having many people take turns at being GM; For long-term campaigns of this nature, it would be a very good idea to discuss your adapted rules and content (commonly called “homebrew” by tabletop RPG enthusiasts) with the rest of your group.
